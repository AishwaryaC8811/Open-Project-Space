The Universe

Dark Energy, Dark Matter, and Ordinary Matter

Dark Energy

Around 13.8 billion years ago, the universe began to expand rapidly which astronomers call the big bang. Dark energy is the current name that astronomers gave the ‘something’ that had caused the universe to expand at an accelerated rate.

There are four main leading explanations on dark energy. One is vacuum energy. Some scientists think that dark energy is the fundamental, always present background energy in space which could be equal to the mathematical term Albert Einstein added to his equation of general relativity.
The next idea is quintessence. Some scientists think that dark energy can be a type of energy fluid or or field that fills space. This idea of dark energy has been nicknamed quintessence after the theoretical fifth element discussed by ancient Greek philosophers.
The next idea of dark energy is space wrinklers. Some scientists think that dark energy could be a defect in the fabric of the universe itself. The last explanation for what dark energy is, is that it is a flaw in general relativity. Some scientists believe that dark energy isn’t something physical that we can discover but rather an issue with general relativity and Einstein's theory of gravity. With this, scientists think that we can adjust our understanding of gravity in such a way that it compliments the observations of the universe without dark energy.

Ordinary Matter

Normal matter takes up everything we can actually observe in the universe. We can see ordinary matter in visible light through our own eyes or through telescopes that detect light we can not see (Ultraviolet and infrared). Most of the normal matter is made up of atomic particles, protons, electrons, and neutrons. It can exist throughout the universe as gas, solid, liquid or even plasma of charged particles. Plasma is the fourth state of matter that is formed when a gas is exposed to strong electromagnetic fields that cause the atoms to split. Though ordinary matter is everywhere around us and in the universe, it only makes up less than 5% of the universe.

Dark Matter

Dark matter is “invisible glue” that holds the universe together. This material that scientists study is all around us. Dark matter makes up most of the matter in the universe. In fact, it makes up the majority of mass in galaxies and galaxy clusters. Scientists also estimate that regular matter takes up only about 5% of the universe while dark matter makes up about 27% of the universe. The rest of the area is thought to be dark energy. Dark matter can interact with ordinary matter through gravity but it can not interact with the electromagnetic spectrum. This means that dark matter does not absorb, reflect, or emit light. Though dark matter is invisible, it takes up space in the universe and holds mass just like ordinary matter. Currently, there are several explanations for dark matter. Scientists also may think that dark matter is made up of more than one particle. The first idea is WIMPs(Weakly Interacting Massive Particles). WIMPs are hypothetical particles that are large and heavy. They can not absorb or emit light. Scientists believe that these particles interact with gravity and maybe even other forces in such a way that allows the WIMPs to pass through normal matter almost perfectly.
Another idea is axions. Axions are hypothetical subatomic particles that are low mass and low energy. Scientists believe that these particles would solve the CP problem. The CP problem is that when regular matter particles are swapped for antimatter particles, the laws of physics should remain the same as well as that the particles should be symmetrical and cancel each other out. Scientists believe that axions would solve the CP problem by violating the symmetry and allow regular matter to grow. The last idea is Primordial black holes. Black holes are the most dense objects in the universe and they have gravity so extreme that at some point, not even light can escape. Primordial black holes are hypothetical black holes that scientists think were formed right after the birth of the universe. One idea with the primordial black holes is that they were formed of regular regular matter at the start of the universe but then collapsed, becoming the dark matter that has now spread across the universe.

Dark matter is an unknown type of matter that holds the galaxies together while dark energy is the name scientists have given to whatever may be causing our universe to expand at a quickening rate over time.
