The Kuiper Belt

The Kuiper Belt is a asteroid belt that is made up of millions of small, icy objects that are leftovers from our solar system's early years.

Not much is known about the Kuiper belt since we are just beginning to explore it and many people mix it up with The Oort Cloud.

But, we do know some about it's dwarf planets (Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, and Eris).

Scroll down to learn more about these planets! (Honorable Ceres mention at the end)

Pluto

Probably the most known dwarf planet, Pluto was a pretty big controversy in the early 2000's.

For decades, it had been considered another planet in our solar system. But, after scientists discovered more dwarf planets in 2006, Pluto's title of planet was revoked and it is now known as a "dwarf planet".

Pluto is half the width of the United states and is 1/5 the width of Earth.

Pluto's orbit is 248 Earth years and has only recently finished it's first full orbit around the moon since it's discovery!


Conducive to life?: Pluto is not conducive to life.

Moons: Pluto has 5 known moons.

Rings: Pluto has no known rings.

Namesake: Pluto was named after the Roman God of the Underworld. (below)

Haumea

Haumea is a very strange dwarf planet. It spins so fast that it is the shape of a football.

Haumea is 1/7 the width of Earth and takes 285 Earth years to orbit the sun.


Conducive to life?: Haumea does not appear to have the conditions suitable for life.

Moons: Haumea has 2 known moons

Rings: Haumea is the first known object in the Kuiper belt to have rings

Namesake: Haumea is named after the Hawaiian Goddess of Fertility. (below)
