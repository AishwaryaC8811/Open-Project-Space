Planets in our Solar System

Our Sun

Although this is included in the planets section of this webpage, the sun is actually not a planet.

And even though it seems huge, our sun isn't actually as big compared to other stars and is fairly young.

In the star life cycle of a sun below, you can see our star is actually only in it's second stage of life.

Conducive to life?: No, the sun is not conducive to life due to it's extreme heat.

Moons: The Sun has no moons

Rings: No rings at the moment

Namesake: Evolved from the Old English word sunne

Mercury

Contrary to popular belief, Mercury actually isn't the hottest planet in the Solar System, even if it is the closest to the Sun.

Mercury is the smallest planet in our Solar System. In fact, it is 1/3 of the Earth's size and spins perfectly upright, giving it no seasons.

Mercury spins extremely slowly. One day on Mercury is 59 Earth days. One day on Mercury is two years on it.


Conducive to life?: Due to Mercury's extremely hot days and extremely cold nights, Mercury is not conducive to life.

Moons: Mercury has no moons.

Rings: Mercury has no rings.

Namesake: Mercury was named after the Roman God of commerce and travel. (Below)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Venus

Did you know Venus is the third brightest object in the sky? It also holds the title as the hottest planet in our Solar System (872 degrees Farenheit), the one planet named after a goddess, and "Earth's Evil Twin".

Venus spins extremely slowly in the opposite direction as us. So slowly in fact one day on Venus (243 Earth days) is longer than one year on Venus (225 Earth days) and it is 117 Earth days between sunrise and sunset.

Venus rotates at a 3 degree tilt, giving it basically no noticable seasons.


Conducive to life?: Could potentially harbor some type of life in it's clouds.

Moons: Has one quasi-moon named Zoozve which is close to Earth, but not threatening.

Rings: Venus has no rings

Namesake: Venus is the only planet named after a goddess. It was named after the Roman god of love and beauty (below)

Earth

Recognize this planet? You should because this is your home planet, meaning I shouldn't have to teach you much about it.

Earth is the only planet in our solar system with confirmed signs of life.


Conducive to life?: Do I actually have to answer this? (yes it is).

Moons: Has one moon named "The Moon"...

Rings: Earth currently has no rings

Namesake: Earth wasn't named after any God. It takes it's name from Ancient German and Old English Words like eorþe

Mars

Mars is the most explored planet (besides Earth).

Mars rotates on a 25 degree tilt, giving it more distinct and longer seasons.

One day on Mars is similar to one Earth day and the planet half the size of Earth.


Conducive to life?: Mars is not currently conducive to life, but scientists are looking for ancient signs of life.

Moons: Mars has two potato-shaped moons named after the horses who pulled the chariot of the God of War (Phobos and Deimos).

Rings: Mars currently has no rings

Namesake: Mars was named after the Roman God of War. (below)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Jupiter

One of my personal favorites, Jupiter is definitely one of the most iconic planets. With it's red storm, which can fit one Earth in it, and being the largest planet in our solar system, it's pretty hard to miss!

Jupiter has 95 moons and spins nearly upright. It also has the shortest day in the solar system and one year on Jupiter is 12 Earth years.

Jupiter is the most volcanically active body in the solar system.


Conducive to life?: While Jupiter itself is not conducive to life as we know it, it's speculated that some of it's moons are likely to harbor life.

Moons: Jupiter has 95 known moons, one of the most any planet has in our solar system (but it isn't the planet with the most).

Rings: Jupiter has very thin, difficult to spot, rings.

Namesake: Jupiter is named after the Roman King of Gods. (below)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Saturn

As my personal favorite, Saturn is one of the most interesting planets in our solar system. With it's spectacular rings that extend 175 miles from itself and it's striking surface, it definitely stands out from all of the others.

Saturn is made mostly of hydrogen and helium and experiences very distinct seasons.

One day on Saturn is 10.7 hours on earth and one year on Saturn is 29.4 Earth years.


Conducive to life?: Saturn is not conducive to life.

Moons: Saturn has 294 moons, making it the planet with the most moons.

Rings: Saturn has the most spectacular rings in the solar system, extending hundreds of miles from the planet.

Namesake: Saturn is named after the Roman god of agriculture and wealth. (below)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Uranus

Uranus is tilted at a 90 degree angle, likely from a massive hit millions of years ago, giving it the most extreme seasons.

Uranus is 4 times wider than Earth and is super cold and windy.

One day on Uranus and 17 hours on Earth and one year on Uranus is 84 Earth years.


Conducive to life?: Uranus is not conducive to life.

Moons: Uranus has 28 known moons

Rings: Uranus has two sets of rings

Namesake: Uranus was named after the greek god of the sky. (below)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Neptune

And finally, we reach our last planet in our solar system. Neptune is one of the most interesting planets in our solar system. In fact, it was located through mathematics and is not visible by the naked eye!

Neptune is four times wider than Earth and even has similar seasons to it.

One day on Neptune is 16 hours on Earth and one year on Neptune is 165 Earth years.


Conducive to life?: Neptune is not conducive to life.

Moons: Neptune has 16 known moons.

Rings: Neptune has very thin rings.

Namesake: Neptune is named after the Roman god of the sea. (below)
